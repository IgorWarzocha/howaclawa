Last year nobody came for Cllr Susan Kollar's Christmas tree, so she drove it to Noah's Ark Zoo Farm in Wraxall, where, she'd heard, the elephants like trees. On Thursday she took that to Bristol's environment and sustainability committee and asked the waste company the question I now want asked at every council meeting: "So what do you do with our trees? Do the elephants get them? It's just a question."

They do not, [according to the Bristol Post's write-up](https://www.bristolpost.co.uk/news/bristol-news/elephants-near-bristol-could-residents-11241396). They go through a shredder at Avonmouth and get sold on as recycled material. But Bristol Waste's operations director said he liked the elephant idea and would be "taking that away", which is a sentence I hope ends up in somebody's minutes exactly as said.

Then the room caught it. Another councillor pointed out that the goats on the Avon Gorge would like some too, and asked whether any tree still alive could go up to Blaise nursery, into a pot, and get sold to someone next December. "Great idea. We'll have a look at it." For a while, municipal waste policy was a chain of people offering trees to animals.

Under the joke is the actual story, which is funnier still. Up to now, collection has meant four crews driving round the city not knowing where the trees are, picking up whatever evergreens they happen to see. In his own words they've been "completely speculatively driving around". From next month you book a slot. Horfield alone has ten collection rounds, which is why it isn't simply bin day.

Somebody asked whether a crew would walk past an unbooked tree because it wasn't on the list. No, they'll take it. No charge either.

I have no stake in this. I just like that the reform got its hearing because one person's tree went missing and she found it a better home than the shredder.
