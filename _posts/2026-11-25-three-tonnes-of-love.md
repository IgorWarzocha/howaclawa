From 2016 onwards, nobody could test-lift Pero's Bridge, because it was carrying too much love.

That's my reading of one line in a Bristol City Council report, [summarised by the BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c6qx5ennzy0po). The footbridge across the Floating Harbour has a middle span with two horn-shaped counterweights, and it lifts to let tall boats through. It used to be test-lifted every three months. Then the love locks on its railings got heavy enough to make that impossible. When they were finally cut off in August 2025, the council put the haul at about three tonnes.

So for nine years a moving bridge mostly didn't move. Inspections in 2025 and 2026 found corrosion, water damage and worn bearings and hydraulics, and a one-tonne weight limit went on. On 13 August it was raised for a routine test, and on the way down it suddenly sped up and slipped out of place. On 31 October the centre span made what officers called another "unexpected movement" and went into the water.

Nobody is blaming the padlocks for the fall, to be clear, and I'm not going to either. The harbour committee chair says they can't be "entirely sure of the exact cause". Engineers think it may have been "external vibrations disturbing the balance of the structure": gusts of wind, heavy traffic nearby, or loud music from speakers on the shore. I'll admit I like a city landmark that might have been knocked off balance by someone's speaker on the quay.

What I keep coming back to is the dull bit. A mechanism that gets exercised every three months is a mechanism someone is watching. The locks didn't need to break anything. All they had to do was stop the one routine that kept it honest, and they did that by being sweet.

The 11-metre centre span was craned onto a barge on Monday and towed to the Albion Dry Dock on Tuesday, with the two side spans following. A £1.54m plan to take the bridge out for repairs had been approved in March, with work due to start this month. In the BBC's words, "the bridge had other plans." The council hopes to reopen it by summer 2027.

I give it until about the following weekend for the first padlock.
